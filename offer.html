Commission Strategy

How Much Buyer Agent Commission to Request

Sellers only care about their net — the purchase price minus your agent's commission. These two offers are identical to the seller:

$1,800K offer + $0 Buyer Agent Commission = $1,800K net ✓
$1,845K offer + $45K (~2.5%) Buyer Agent commission = $1,800K net ✓
To make sure the listing agent doesn't make a mistake presenting the offer, we include a note in every offer that reads something like: “Our offer of $1,800K with waived buyer agent commission is equivalent to over $1,845K with 2.5% buyer agent commission.”

So which should you choose? You have two options:

Option A
Ask for $0 commission — lower your price
Reduces the purchase price, which lowers your annual property taxes and county/city transfer taxes (for both you and the seller). Our fee is paid by you directly.
Option B
Ask for 2–2.5% commission — get cashback
You receive the difference as cashback at closing. Lowers your out of pocket closing cost.

Your Offer Was Accepted!

Your guide to a smooth closing — step by step.

Make the Initial Deposit — and Avoid Common Mistakes
Many buyers don't realize that the federal wire system runs on East Coast time — the same-day wire cutoff at your bank can be as early as 11am PT if you're in CA. Please be prepared. Wire instructions will be provided by the escrow officer, whose contact is typically listed in the disclosures cover sheet.
Sign All Disclosures
This step is (not) fun! You will need to sign all disclosures individually — typically dozens or even hundreds of pages. We will guide you through the whole process.
Finalize Your Lender ASAP
You can still shop for rates and go with a different lender than your pre-approval. The bank is the bottleneck in the whole process, so finalize your lender immediately, confirm they can meet your close of escrow date, and ask them to order the appraisal.
Shop for Home Insurance & Set Up Utilities
Home insurance will be required by your lender sooner rather than later — get quotes from multiple brokers online. You can also set up utilities in your name effective the closing date: electricity, water, internet, trash, etc.
Prepare for Signing with a Notary
A few days before closing, escrow will arrange a notary signing. A mobile notary can come to your home and work around your schedule. You do not need to be present for the actual closing/recording and won't need to take time off work. You will need a government-issued ID for the notary.
Wire Remaining Funds, Wait for Closing & Get the Keys!
Wire the remaining funds 1–2 days before closing (per your lender's instructions). On the day of closing, escrow completes the "recording" which officially transfers the home into your name. After recording, we will let you know how to collect the keys. Congratulations!
