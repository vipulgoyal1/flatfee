Los Angeles, CA

    The entertainment capital of the world and the economic center of Southern California.

Los Angeles is a massive, engineered city: it’s both flat and hilly, split by the Santa Monica Mountains (Basin vs. Valley), and it grew into a world city because infrastructure made it possible—most famously the Los Angeles Aqueduct (1913) bringing water from Eastern California.

LA’s housing demand is powered by two engines that never went away: entertainment and global trade. It’s a global hub for film/TV and home to one of the busiest container-port complexes in the Americas (Port of LA / Long Beach), so job centers are spread across the city, not concentrated in one downtown.


Los Angeles Real Estate Trends

Like most places in CA, Los Angeles experienced a mild price correction and ended 2025 cooler on paper. Los Angeles is not a blanket bidding-war market—it's a pricing-and-condition market. Zillow reports a 0.991 median sale-to-list ratio—buyers have leverage when a home is stale, mispriced, or stays on the market for long. However the prime inventory still moves fast and multiple bids within a week on the market are still very common.
